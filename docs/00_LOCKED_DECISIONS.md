## 19. Phase 1 validation protocol

**Written before the data exists, deliberately.** Interpretation rules decided in advance are the only defence against reading a story into noise at day 20.

### The test objective

**Not:** did the business succeed · did the product sell · did Pinterest explode.

**The only question:** *can a beginner-focused free tracker attract qualified subscribers from Pinterest?*

### Interpretation rules

#### Scenario 1 — Near-zero impressions
*20–300 impressions after several weeks, almost no clicks.*

**This is the most likely month-one outcome. It is not a failure signal.**

Likely causes: new account distribution · no keyword history · few trust signals.

**Do:** keep publishing · expand keyword coverage · improve board descriptions · keep the templates unchanged.

**Do not:** redesign the brand · change the lead magnet · rewrite all headlines. The temptation to change everything at day 20 is the single most destructive thing you can do to this test, because it destroys the baseline you're trying to establish.

#### Scenario 2 — Impressions but low clicks
Pinterest is showing the content; the hook is weak.

Review: headline clarity · keyword alignment · thumbnail readability · visual hierarchy.

**Change one variable per batch, not per pin.** At 5–10 pins/week you cannot run clean A/B tests — the volume isn't there. Change one thing across a week's pins, compare to the previous week, and treat the read as **directional, not conclusive.** Claiming statistical significance at this volume would be self-deception.

#### Scenario 3 — Clicks but low opt-ins
The pin promise works; the landing page promise doesn't.

Review: landing page headline · tracker preview · trust elements · form friction.

**Do not change Pinterest strategy yet.** The stage that failed is the stage to fix.

#### Scenario 4 — Opt-ins but low engagement
The magnet attracts; the relationship doesn't form.

Review: Email 1 delivery and placement (inbox vs. promotions vs. spam) · download experience on mobile · survey placement · sequence timing.

**Check the Day 0 download click-rate first.** If subscribers aren't downloading, nothing downstream can work.

#### Scenario 5 — Survey responses
The audience is telling you what to build. **With one guard.**

**Separate the two kinds of signal:**

| Signal | Useful at | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| **Replies** — her actual words | n = 5 | Language, objections, copy, hypotheses |
| **Option distribution** — which button won | Higher n | Deciding what to build |

**The risk is concrete:** the budget system is a 30–50 hour build. Committing that on the strength of four clicks versus two is not listening to the audience, it's rounding noise into a mandate. A two-response gap at low volume is noise.

**Rule:** treat early distribution as hypothesis-generating. Read replies immediately and act on the *language* straight away — that's free and low-risk. Wait for real volume before letting the distribution pick the product.

Never ignore qualitative data because numbers are small. Never mistake a small distribution for a verdict.

## 20. Globalization rules — IMMUTABLE

**Check this section before writing any customer-facing word.** These exist because "use neutral English" as a principle lost repeatedly to concrete regional values that were already written down somewhere. Same fix as the founder-voice and naming-the-judgment lists: move the rule from guidance into a checkable list.

### The six rules

1. **US spelling in all customer-facing copy** — not only keywords. *Organize, color, favorite, center, analyze.* Mixed spelling within one page is worse than either choice consistently.
2. **Internationally neutral vocabulary always.** Where a word differs by country, pick the form understood everywhere or avoid the word.
3. **No country-specific financial terminology.** No 401(k), ISA, RRSP, superannuation, IRS, HMRC, Social Security, EIN, sort code, routing number.
4. **No country-specific support organizations** unless the page is explicitly country-targeted.
5. **Every printable designed for Letter and A4 from one master layout.** Master canvas **8.27 × 11 in**, live area **7.27 × 10 in**.
6. **Assume multiple currencies.** No currency symbols in printables or templates. No example amounts in a single currency.

### The regional-word list

| Never | Use |
|---|---|
| gas | fuel |
| takeout / takeaway | delivery, eating out |
| mom, mama | *(banned anyway — `04` §7)* |
| checkbook, check | avoid entirely |
| diaper / nappy | baby supplies |
| stroller / pram | baby gear |
| closet | storage |
| vacation | trip, time off |
| trash / rubbish | avoid entirely |
| faucet, bangs, sidewalk, cell phone | avoid entirely |
| 211, NFCC, Citizens Advice | see rule 4 |
| "the average American household" | avoid all national averages |

### The screening question

> **Would this word, figure or organization mean the same thing to a reader in Ohio, Manchester, Melbourne and Toronto?**

If not, change it. If unsure, avoid it.

## 21. When a locked decision may change

**Default: it doesn't.** Everything in this file stays locked unless one of six triggers fires. That preserves the integrity of the experiment — changing several variables before collecting meaningful data means learning nothing from any of them.

| Trigger | Example |
|---|---|
| **A verification gate fails** | Prata isn't available in Canva |
| **A usability test fails** | Readers consistently misread a category |
| **Analytics contradict an assumption** | The lowest-priority pin angle produces the highest opt-in rate |
| **An external platform changes** | Kit removes a free-tier feature the funnel depends on |
| **An internal contradiction surfaces** | A global market makes a US-Letter-only printable incoherent |
| **Operator override** | A deliberate strategic change with no external trigger. Must be recorded as such, never as a refinement |

The fifth is the one that fired most often while these files were being written, and it fires without any evidence arriving — a decision made under an assumption outlives the assumption. It gets its own trigger because the other four all wait for the world to tell you something.

**Procedure, whichever fires:** update the source file first, then this registry, in the same move. Record the date and the trigger. A change that lives in only one of the two is drift, and drift is what this file exists to prevent.
