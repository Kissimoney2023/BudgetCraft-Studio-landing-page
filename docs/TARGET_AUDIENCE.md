## 1. Primary avatar

**Sarah, 32.**

- Married or living with a partner
- Mother of one or two young children
- Works full-time or part-time; may have recently returned from maternity leave
- **Income: qualitative, not a figure.** A household with enough coming in to cover essential
  bills most months, but where discretionary spending regularly feels difficult to explain.
  Deliberately not anchored to one country's salary scale (`00` §20, rule 6)
- Busy with family and household responsibilities; time-poor rather than lazy
- Wants simple systems that save time, not complex financial strategies

Sarah is the **primary avatar, not the only permitted customer.** Content is written *for* her and
remains useful to anyone with similar needs — men, single women, couples without children,
students, young professionals are not excluded.

**Consequence for copy:** address the reader as **"you."** Do not default to "hey mama," "for busy
moms," or similar. The mother-of-young-children context belongs in the *examples* (kids'
activities, the grocery run, the one-income month), not in how the reader is greeted. Sarah still
recognises herself; nobody else is shut out.

## 2. Financial situation

- Regular paycheck, little left at month end
- Covers essentials, does not feel financially secure
- Keeps meaning to budget, struggles to stay consistent
- Some consumer debt: credit cards, car loan, possibly student loans
- Frequent everyday purchases on Amazon and other online retailers
- Shops on Amazon and Etsy

**What she wants in the next 12 months (her goals, in her framing):** build a first emergency
fund · stop living paycheck to paycheck · stick to a monthly budget · pay off one major debt ·
open her banking app without anxiety · spend intentionally without guilt

These are recorded as **Sarah's aspirations, not BudgetCraft Studio promises.** No product, pin,
email or blog post may imply she will reach any of them. No income claims, no guaranteed savings
amounts.

**Primary goal the brand organises around:** *control of monthly spending.*

Note the deliberate split — she *states* the emergency fund and the house; she would *pay today*
to fix "where did my money go." Hooks lead with the spending-control pain. The emergency fund is
positioned as the downstream outcome.

## 3. The trigger moment

**End-of-month shortfall.** She reconciles, or simply notices, and the number is worse than she
expected.

Three consequences:

1. **Her search language is retrospective and mildly self-blaming**, not aspirational. She types
   "where does my money go," not "how to save for a house."
2. **The publishing rhythm is the last week of every month through roughly the 4th** — twelve
   small peaks a year, not one January bet. Better fit for ~10 hrs/week than a seasonal scramble.
3. **The free product must diagnose, not plan.** A tracker that shows her the leak answers the
   question she just typed. Goal-setting worksheets do not.

## 4. THE CORE CUSTOMER BELIEF

**Locked. This is the single most load-bearing statement in the file.**

The barrier is not lack of information. It is: **"I always start, but I never finish."**

Sarah does not believe she is *incapable* of budgeting. She believes she cannot **maintain a
system**. That distinction rewrites the product philosophy.

**Not selling:** a perfect financial system · a complicated budgeting method · a spreadsheet
replacement

**Selling:** **a simple money routine she can restart anytime.**

Everything in §7 (why the spreadsheet failed), §11 (objections) and the whole of `BRAND_VOICE`
descends from this line. If a product, pin or email does not survive contact with it, it is wrong
regardless of how well it is executed.

## 5. Ranked pain points

Ranked by operator judgement. No numeric scores — the order is the signal.

**Critical — the launch core**

1. **"I don't know where my money goes every month."** Earns enough to cover bills, surprised by
   what's left. *"I know I should have more money left over."*
2. **"I keep trying to budget, but I never stick with it."** Good intentions, drift by week two.
   Needs a simpler system, not more motivation.

**1 and 2 are one sequence, not two problems.** #1 is what she feels at month end; #2 is what
happens three weeks after she tries to fix it. The *tracker* answers the first half; the *format*
(weekly, pre-filled, 10–15 min) answers the second. Do not build two competing products here.

3. **"I don't have time for complicated budgeting."** Wants something usable in 10–15 minutes a
   week.
4. **"Financial advice feels confusing."** Wants clear language, simple steps, printable tools,
   real-life examples.

**High — natural later expansions**

5. "I have no emergency savings." Car repairs, medical bills, school and home costs each feel
   like a setback.
6. "Bills keep catching me by surprise." Missed due dates, late fees, forgotten annual
   subscriptions.
7. "I'm saving for goals, but I keep spending the money." Vacations, Christmas, a future home,
   family experiences.

**Parked — not at launch**

8. "Debt feels like it's controlling my life." Real, but edges toward advice this brand does not
   give.
9. "Money conversations make me anxious." Avoids balances and statements — not from apathy, from
   overwhelm. Needs more care than a beginner printable can carry.

**Not a product at all**

10. "I want to feel financially confident." This is the brand promise. It belongs in the voice
    file, not the shop.

## 7. What she has already tried, and why it failed

### RESOLVED — Excel / Google Sheets only

**Tried: a spreadsheet budget template. Only that.**

She has **not** tried YNAB, Mint, EveryDollar, cash stuffing, or an abandoned paper planner. She
is **spreadsheet-scarred, not app-fatigued.**

**The copy consequence, and it is strict.** Sarah's posture is *"I tried once, it was over my
head, I quietly stopped"* — a slightly embarrassed beginner, not a cynical veteran.

**Never write:** "you've downloaded every budgeting app and none of them stuck" · "you've tried
everything" · "another planner that'll end up in a drawer." That addresses a woman who has failed
many times. Sarah has failed *once*, at one specific thing, and she is quietly ashamed of it.
Copy that assumes a graveyard of failed attempts tells her she is in the wrong room.

**Do write:** "simpler than the spreadsheet that defeated you." One named enemy, one honest
promise.

Note the interaction with §4. Her belief *"I always start, but I never finish"* is real, but it is
generalised from a **single** money-system failure plus ordinary life friction — not from a long
history of abandoned budgeting tools. Honour the belief; do not invent the evidence for it.

### Why the spreadsheet failed — two reasons, in order

1. **Too complicated to set up.** She stalled at the blank template.
2. **It worked, but the news was bad.** A second attempt reached a number that made her want to
   close the laptop.

### The two hard design constraints that follow

**Zero configuration.** Every product ships pre-filled with categories a household recognises on
sight — groceries, fuel, kids' activities, subscriptions, Amazon, eating out. No blank category
lists. No "customise this to your situation." That step is what killed her. Wherever there is a
decision to make, print the default on the page instead.

**The diagnosis must not read as a verdict.** No red ink. No deficit totals in scary type. No
scoring. No "you should be spending X% on groceries" benchmarks. Output is *information* —
category totals plus a single prompt such as "which number surprised you most?" Language of
noticing, not judging.

### Positioning that falls out of this

**Find out where your money goes without feeling terrible about it.**

Most competitors do the first half only.

**One named enemy: the spreadsheet.** "No spreadsheets" is not a generic differentiator here — it
is *the* promise, and it is honest, so it can go in pin titles, blog intros and product
descriptions without hedging.

## 9. The product ladder

**Channel:** BudgetCraft Studio's own shop, with the email list behind it.

Accepted tradeoff: better margin and customer ownership, but discovery is entirely self-generated
— nobody browses an independent shop the way they browse Etsy — plus checkout, file delivery,
refunds and sales tax on digital goods. **The shop only works if the Pinterest → email funnel
works.** That is a dependency, not a parallel track. A thin Etsy presence purely for discovery is
a phase-two question, not ruled out.

### Ladder as locked in v2

| Rung | Product | Price | Job |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free | Budget Starter Tracker — *"Where Did My Money Go?"* | $0 | **Diagnose** the leak · capture the email |
| 1 | Simple printable tools | **$7–$12** | Convert subscriber → customer at low risk |
| 2 | Complete budget systems | **$19–$39** | **Transformation.** The core offer |
| 3 | Premium bundles | **$49–$99** | Raise average order value |
| 4 | Membership | **$10–$15/month** | Retention · home for Canva-editable files |

### RESOLVED — confirmed by operator

- **Rung 1 ($7–$12) = single-purpose sheets.** One job each: a bill tracker, a savings challenge,
  a spending audit. Small page counts. This is the impulse rung — the job is *converting a
  subscriber into a customer*, not making money.
- **Rung 2 ($19–$39) = the monthly budget system.** Pre-filled, weekly rhythm, 10–15 minutes, no
  configuration, undated, restart page. **This is the core offer** and the thing the email
  sequence is built to sell.

This uses the channel advantage the shop was chosen for, while keeping a genuinely cheap first
purchase for a beginner who got burned once and is wary of paying again.

**Build consequence — plan for it.** The core product is a substantially bigger build than a
sub-$10 product would have been: higher page count, real written instructions, more design hours,
and a sales page that has to justify $19–$39 rather than an impulse click. Against ~10 hrs/week,
expect the core system to be a multi-week build. **Sequence suggestion: ship the free tracker and
one rung-1 sheet first** — they prove the funnel works before you sink weeks into the flagship.

### The free/paid boundary — locked

| | Purpose | Answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Free lead magnet** | **Diagnosis** | "Where is my money going?" |
| **Paid products** | **Transformation** | "What system will help me manage my money every week?" |

Free examples: expense tracker · spending audit · money leak checklist
Paid examples: complete budget planner · monthly money system · savings challenge bundle ·
financial organisation kit

**If the freebie creeps into being a system, the shop competes with itself.** Say explicitly what
the free tracker deliberately does *not* do.

## 10. Format decision — LOCKED

**Two files per product at launch. Not six.**

| Format | Ship at launch | Reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Printable PDF**, US Letter 8.5×11" | **Yes** | Designed for home printing |
| **Digital PDF**, tablet-sized | **Yes** | Serves GoodNotes, Notability, all annotation apps, desktop viewing — there is no proprietary "GoodNotes format," they import PDFs |
| Canva-editable | **No** | Reduces product protection · increases support issues · better suited to membership |
| Fillable PDF | **No** | **Canva cannot create PDF form fields.** Requires an additional paid tool · validate demand first |
| PNG/JPG exports | **No** | Additional production work · no proven demand |

**Why two and not six:** each extra format is a version to redesign, re-proof, re-upload, rename
and fix when a typo appears on page 4. Six formats at ~10 hrs/week is scope paralysis wearing the
costume of thoroughness.

> labelled download links**, not one — see `docs/welcome-sequence-draft.md`. A single link cannot
> serve both, and the labelled click doubles as a behavioural signal on the printing question
> below.

## 11. Objections and required product responses

### Objection 1 — "I'll quit by week three." *(primary)*

**Root cause: identity, not price.** She has evidence from previous attempts and concludes *"the
problem is me. I never stick with anything."* This cannot be argued with. It is disarmed by
format.

**Required features — every product, no exceptions:**

**Undated pages.** A missed month must not make the planner feel wasted. → Not "January Budget."
Use **"Monthly Budget."**

**A restart page.** Every product includes **"Missed a Week? Start Here"**, carrying the message:
*"You don't need to restart perfectly. Just continue from today."*

**No failure mechanics.** No long streak counters. No punishment tracking. No "perfect month"
badges. No scoring systems. → **Encourage consistency, not perfection.**

### Objection 2 — "I can probably find a free tracker online."

A valid concern, and partly self-inflicted: BudgetCraft Studio's own free tracker creates it.
Answered by positioning, not by argument — the free/paid boundary in §9. State what the free
tracker deliberately does not do.

### Not raised by the operator

Price sensitivity, email reluctance, format uncertainty. **Do not write copy against objections
she does not have.**

## 12. Who this is explicitly not for

Boundaries exist so the brand does not drift into generic money advice.

**1. People in severe financial crisis.** Eviction risk, food insecurity, immediate emergencies,
collections or legal debt, homelessness. These products are financial *organisation and habit
building*, not crisis intervention.

**Handling rule:** where content brushes against genuine crisis, BudgetCraft Studio **signposts to
real help and stops.** It does not offer "general educational resources" to someone facing
eviction.

**Global formulation (updated 30 July 2026):** encourage the reader to contact an appropriate
local debt-advice, financial counselling, or community support service in their country. **Do not
name country-specific organizations** unless the page is explicitly targeted at that country.
Naming US services such as 211 or the NFCC to a global audience is both wrong for most readers and
a maintenance burden across dozens of regional variants.

If a country-specific page is ever written, verify every named service is current before
publishing.

Never position the brand as a substitute for financial counsellors, government assistance or debt
specialists.

**2. Advanced investing.** No stock picking, crypto, trading, options, index fund strategy,
retirement portfolio optimisation, wealth building.

**3. High-income wealth optimisation.** No tax strategy, luxury planning, six-figure income
management, complex wealth systems.

**4. Debt payoff as a specialism.** Debt content may appear where it connects to budgeting. The
brand does not become a debt elimination, credit repair or debt coaching business.
✅ "Debt payoff tracker printable" · ❌ "The ultimate debt freedom coaching program"

**5. Pure motivation and aesthetic money content.** No quote graphics, luxury lifestyle
inspiration, get-rich content, manifestation-only content, money wallpapers, or vision boards
without practical systems. Sarah needs tools. *(This is also why the "stop spending" keyword lane
was dropped — see §8.)*

**6. Business and freelance finances.** No bookkeeping, freelance taxes, LLC finances, invoicing
or revenue tracking. Possible separate future line if demand appears.

## 13. Boundary statement and content filter

**This brand is for** people who have income coming in but feel disorganised, overwhelmed or
inconsistent with managing money. They want simple budgeting systems, printable tools, clear
financial organisation, better saving habits, and more confidence with everyday money decisions.

**This brand is not for** people seeking professional financial advice, investment strategies,
emergency financial assistance, business finance systems, or advanced wealth management.

**Positioning:** BudgetCraft Studio helps everyday people create simple money systems that reduce
stress and build confidence — one budget, tracker and financial habit at a time.

### The filter every product, post and pin must pass

**"Will this help Sarah feel more confident, organised, or in control of her money today?"** If
no, it does not belong in the business.

And: *"Would Sarah understand this? Would Sarah save this pin? Would Sarah buy this planner?"*

**Good fit:** "How to create a monthly budget you can actually follow" · "30-day savings challenge
printable" · "Simple bill tracker for busy families" · "How to organise your finances in 15
minutes a week"

**Bad fit:** "Best stocks to buy in 2026" · "How millionaires invest" · "Crypto investing
strategy" · "How to build a seven-figure business"

**Standing constraints:** educational content and organisational tools only. No financial advice.
No income claims. No guaranteed savings. Affiliate relationships always disclosed; commission
rates and programme terms never invented.
