## 1. Pinterest's role

> **Pinterest earns attention. The landing page earns the email. Email earns the sale.**

Pinterest is the only discovery channel at launch, which makes it the single point of failure for
the whole funnel. If pins don't produce opt-ins, no downstream work matters — which is why
`LEAD_MAGNET_SYSTEM` §11 puts audience validation in Phase 1 and defers the paid product to
Phase 2.

**Pinterest is not used for:** selling directly · building a following as an end in itself · brand
awareness without a click · community engagement.

Every pin exists to move Sarah one step: pin → landing page → email.

| 2 | budget for beginners printables |

### Required first task: keyword expansion

**Five phrases will not fill a month of distinct pins.** With a deliberately narrow visual system,
keyword *selection* replaces keyword *volume* as the lever — there is no brute-forcing your way to
the right phrase.

Before the first pin, run the autocomplete capture method from `TARGET_AUDIENCE` §8 across at
least fifteen seed phrases and build a working keyword list. Method: type the seed into the
Pinterest search bar, do not press enter, record every suggestion verbatim including typos.

Seeds to start with: *where did my money go · budget for beginners · monthly budget · spending
tracker · budget printable · money organization · budget planner free · how to budget · track
spending · expense tracker · budget binder · money management for beginners · family budget · bill
tracker · paycheck budget.*

**Do not use the "stop spending" lane** — `TARGET_AUDIENCE` §8 records it as motivation and
phone-wallpaper intent, not tool-seeking.

## 3. Pin design system

### Format — verified

| | Spec |
|---|---|
| Size | **1000 × 1500 px, 2:3 ratio** |
| Max file size | 20 MB (keep well under) |
| Title limit | ~100 characters |
| Never | taller than 2:3 — pins past 1:2.1 get cropped in the feed and the bottom is lost |

### Headline — locked

**Bold humanist sans.** The display serif appears nowhere on a pin (`VISUAL_IDENTITY` §12).
Pinterest is a speed environment; the pin must communicate before Sarah decides whether to stop.

### The keyword must be visible on the pin image

Pinterest analyzes the image itself, including text overlays. The target phrase goes **in the
headline on the pin**, not only in the metadata. This is why the lead magnet is named *Where Did
My Money Go?* — the search phrase, the pin headline and the product name are the same words.

### Renders, not illustration

`VISUAL_IDENTITY` §8 permits product renders only — no illustration, no photography, no AI images.
This is also what the platform appears to reward in this niche: budgeting pins perform better when
they show an actual planner rather than an abstract graphic. The constraint and the best practice
agree.

### The variation matrix — four locked Canva templates

"Visually distinct" is enforceable only if defined. Build four templates; vary within them.

| Axis | Options |
|---|---|
| **Layout** | Headline top · headline bottom · headline beside render · headline over full-width render |
| **Render** | Flat page · angled page · tablet frame · tight crop on the category rows |
| **Terracotta** | Footer strip · vertical bar · headline underline · total-box highlight |

Four layouts × four renders = sixteen genuinely different-looking pins before anything repeats,
all unmistakably BudgetCraft Studio. **Variation becomes selection, not design** — which is the
point of a template system.

**Never rebuild a pin from a blank canvas.** Duplicate a locked template (`VISUAL_IDENTITY` §10).

## 4. Content angles

Four angles for launch. Each pin has exactly one job.

### Angle 1 — Money awareness

*"Help me understand where my money goes."*
Examples: Where Did My Money Go? · Find Your Hidden Spending · See Your Monthly Spending Patterns
**Destination:** landing page. **CTA:** download the free tracker.

### Angle 2 — Beginner budgeting

*"I need a simple place to start."*
Examples: Budgeting For Beginners · First Monthly Budget Template · Simple Budget Routine
**Destination:** landing page or blog post.

### Angle 3 — Consistency problem — RESOLVED

*"I always start, but I never finish."*

**Destination: the free tracker at `/free-budget-tracker`. Not a paid product, not a waitlist.**

The mismatch is solved by using the consistency pain as the **hook** and the process promise as
the **offer** — which is exactly what `BRAND_VOICE` §6 permits at the discovery stage. The pin
names her real problem; the CTA offers the first step toward it.

Approved copy direction:

- *"A budget you don't restart every month starts with knowing where your money goes."*
- *"The easiest first step isn't creating a perfect budget. It's seeing your spending clearly."*
- *"Start with one page. Find your spending patterns."*

**Reasoning, recorded:** consistency is Sarah's deepest problem, but the brand has not earned the
right to sell a "system" before she has experienced one small win. This angle is also the brand's
clearest differentiation — competitors frame the problem as discipline; BudgetCraft Studio frames
it as sustainability.

**Voice check on this angle specifically.** It invites founder voice — *"the tracker I wish I
had"* — which is banned outright by `BRAND_VOICE` §8. Use *"the tracker nobody hands you"* or
similar. No first-person history.

### Angle 4 — Organization

*"I want my finances to feel less chaotic."*
Examples: Organize Your Money Month · Monthly Money Planning · Family Budget Organization
**Destination:** blog post, then landing page.

### Waitlist — REJECTED

**No waitlist at launch.** A waitlist creates a second funnel and an additional promise to
maintain before the core product exists.

**Replaced by** the Day 7 survey question in `LEAD_MAGNET_SYSTEM` §8: *"What would help you most
next?"* Same product-direction signal, no extra funnel, and it doubles as the Phase 1 close for a
sequence that has nothing to sell yet.

### Angle test priority

Order of expected strength, for reading the 40-pin test:

1. **Where Did My Money Go?** — highest alignment. Matches her search language, the end-of-month
   trigger, and the free tracker directly.
2. **Beginner budgeting** — search-driven, large audience, strong printable intent.
3. **Restart anytime / consistency** — brand differentiation.
4. **Organization** — supporting category. Popular on Pinterest, weaker purchase intent.

*Note: US spelling throughout — "Organization," never "Organisation" (`BRAND_VOICE` §7).*

## 6. Publishing system

**Manual publishing for the first 90 days.** Pinterest's own native scheduling is permitted and
encouraged — it is free, enforces spacing, and automates nothing requiring judgment. No paid
scheduler at launch.

**Volume — locked:** minimum sustainable **5 fresh pins/week** · growth target **10/week** ·
ceiling **14/week**.

**No increase until all three are true:** email signups are consistent · winning keywords are
identified · templates are working.

### Why manual, and what it costs

A scheduler creates efficiency and can hide weak signals. Phase 1 is a learning phase, not a
scaling phase — every pin is a small experiment. The cost, stated honestly: pin performance is
lottery-shaped, so fewer pins means fewer tickets. The compensation is that each must be a
considered bet rather than a variant, which is why §2 makes keyword expansion the first task.

### Weekly rhythm

| Day | Work | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Monday** | Research and plan the week's pin list: keyword, angle, destination, CTA | ~1 hr |
| **Tuesday** | Design batch — 5–10 pins from locked templates, rotating layout/render/accent | ~1.5 hrs |
| **Wed–Sun** | Publish. Upload, SEO title, description, correct link, record result | ~10 min/day |

### Publishing rule

Every pin has one job. Not *"get inspired about money."* Yes: *"download the free spending
tracker"* · *"learn how to create a monthly budget"* · *"see the simple weekly money routine."*

### Spacing rules

Space out pins pointing at the same link — do not publish several the same day. One pin, one
board, once.

## 7. The 40-pin validation test

Replaces a slower 100-pin plan, which at this volume would take two to three months to read.

**Phase 1: four angles × ten pins over roughly 30 days.** Then the next 40–60 pins go behind the
winner.

### Metric ladder — pre-committed

**Primary: email signups.**

But a new account at this volume may produce almost no signups in thirty days, and four angles ×
near-zero is not a result. So the fallback order is fixed **now**, before there is any emotional
investment in a particular angle:

1. **Email signups** — use if there are enough to differentiate between angles
2. **Outbound clicks** — use if signups are too sparse
3. **Saves** — use if clicks are too sparse
4. **Extend the window** — if all three are noise, keep running rather than pick a winner from
   three signups versus one

**Do not read meaning into differences of one or two.** Distribution on a new Pinterest account
takes weeks to build; a flat first month is an expected outcome, not a failed test.

### Tracking sheet

Date · Pin title · Keyword · Angle · Board · Template used · Impressions · Saves · Outbound
clicks · Email signups

Google Sheets or Airtable. Recorded at publish time and reviewed weekly.

## 8. Titles and descriptions

### Titles

Keyword-first. Clear beats clever — Pinterest needs relevance, not wordplay. *"Monthly Budget
Printable for Beginners"* not *"This Changed Everything."* Under ~100 characters.

### Descriptions

A sentence or two in natural language that includes the primary keyword and says what happens
after the click. Written as prose, not a keyword list. Hashtags contribute almost nothing to
ranking and are deprioritized.

**Character limit is unverified** — sources cite 800, 500 and "under 100." Check the Pinterest
composer directly; it is the only source that cannot be wrong.

### Voice constraint — non-negotiable

Pin copy follows `BRAND_VOICE` §8. Banned outright: *take charge of your financial future · unlock
your wealth potential · master your money mindset · achieve financial freedom*, and the entire
naming-the-judgment group (*without judgment · judgment-free · no shame · not your spending
mistakes*).

**If an external AI tool writes pin copy, paste `BRAND_VOICE` §8's banned lists into the prompt
every time.** That tool has no access to these files, and the naming-the-judgment phrasing has
already recurred three times in this project *with* the rules in context. Without them it will
default to the influencer register.

## 9. Drift rules

Pinterest content must never become: get-rich content · investing advice · luxury lifestyle
finance · shame-based spending content · *"stop buying coffee"* messaging · money wallpapers,
quote graphics or vision boards (`TARGET_AUDIENCE` §12, `VISUAL_IDENTITY` §11).

Always: practical tools · organization · beginner-friendly systems · confidence through clarity.

Every pin passes the 3-second phone test from `VISUAL_IDENTITY` §6 and the content filter from
`TARGET_AUDIENCE` §13.

## 10. Automation roadmap

**Phase 1 — manual, 0–90 days.** Canva, Pinterest native scheduling, a tracking sheet. Human
keeps: keyword choice, analytics review, deciding winners.

**Phase 2 — assisted, after validation.** Canva bulk create, scheduling, AI-drafted descriptions,
a keyword database.

**Phase 3 — content engine.** Keyword database → briefs → templates → scheduled publishing →
analytics feedback loop.

**Rule: do not automate a process that has not yet been proven.** Automating the wrong workflow is
more expensive than doing it by hand.

## 11. Time budget

**Superseded by `SEO_BLOG_ENGINE` §4, which allocates Pinterest 3.0 hr/week.** That allocation
governs.

Pinterest's 3.0 hours is consistent with 5–10 pins/week: roughly 1 hr planning, 1.5 hr design
batch, 0.5 hr publishing and review.

### The consequence worth naming

**Product development at 1 hr/week of research notes in Phase 1 means the $19–39 system is not
being built yet.** That is deliberate: `SEO_BLOG_ENGINE` §1 governs — *BudgetCraft Studio does not
scale content before the funnel works.* When the funnel is proven and product development resumes
at 3 hr/week, a multi-page system with real written instructions is plausibly 30–50 hours, i.e. a
three-to-four month build.

Two honest implications: the Phase 1 email sequence needs to work *without* a product for several
months (`LEAD_MAGNET_SYSTEM` §8 has a Phase 1 Day 7 for exactly this), and if Pinterest production
runs long in month one, the lever is fewer pins rather than longer days.

## 12. Research provenance

Verified by search on 30 July 2026. Confidence is stated because Pinterest advice is a heavily
monetized content category and most sources sell scheduling tools.

### High confidence — multiple converging sources

1000 × 1500 at 2:3 · tall pins cropped past 1:2.1 · title limit ~100 characters · 20 MB file cap ·
fresh pin designs outperform repins · keywords in titles, descriptions and board names are the
dominant ranking factor · hashtags now near-worthless · Pinterest analyzes the image and text
overlay · keyword-first naming for boards.

### Low confidence — sources contradict

**Posting frequency.** Found 1–5, 3–5, 5–15, 5–25 and 15–25 fresh pins/day, all stated confidently
in 2026. No consensus. The locked 5–10/week in §6 is an operator decision based on sustainable
capacity, **not** a platform recommendation.

**Idea Pins.** One source says merged into a unified Pin format in 2023; another says renamed in
late 2024 with a distinct 9:16 spec; another says they dominate discovery; another says do not
plan them at all. **Check Pinterest's own help center before building anything around them.** For
a traffic business, standard 2:3 pins are reported to drive more outbound clicks regardless.

**Description character limit.** 800 vs 500 vs 100. Unresolved.

### Credibility note

Sources quoted Pinterest's monthly active users as 498M, 553M, 619M and 631M within the same few
months — a reliable tell that this content category recycles itself. Treat any unsourced number in
a Pinterest blog post as unverified.
