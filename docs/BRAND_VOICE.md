## 1. Who is speaking

**A faceless studio brand with a human, supportive personality.**

Not a personal finance influencer. Not an anonymous corporate voice. The speaker is **BudgetCraft
Studio** — a calm, practical friend who happens to be good at organising money systems.

**Not:** a financial guru · a millionaire mentor · a certified financial advisor · a productivity
influencer · a lifestyle personality.

### Pronouns

**"We"** speaks as the brand. **"You"** addresses the customer.

- "We designed every page so you can restart whenever life gets busy."
- "You don't need a complicated spreadsheet."
- "You can start from where you are today."

### The "we" constraint — hard limit

A solo operator using "we" is standard for a studio brand and is permitted. But **"we" may never
imply people, credentials or track record that do not exist.**

Banned: "our team of experts" · "our financial advisors" · "we've helped thousands" · "our
certified planners" · any staff count, any client count.

The voice is plural. The claims stay true.

### No founder story — ever

Never write: *"I used to be terrible with money"* · *"I tried this and it changed my life"* ·
*"I paid off $50,000 of debt."*

These require a real founder story with real financial history. **Do not invent one.** This is
not a stylistic preference; it is a truthfulness rule.

### Reserved name

**"Sarah" is reserved.** It is the primary avatar's name across every file in this project. It
may never be used as a sender name, persona, staff name or testimonial name. If a human sender is
ever introduced, it needs a different name.

### Sender identity

Emails send from **BudgetCraft Studio**. Not "BudgetCraft Studio Team" — "Team" quietly asserts
staff that do not exist.

> **Known live violation, 1 Aug 2026:** the Kit sending address is `sahjohnny@gmail.com` with
> `from_name` set to that literal string. Every sequence email would ship showing a personal
> Gmail address, breaking both this rule and the faceless-studio decision above. See
> `00_LOCKED_DECISIONS` §18.12. Fixable in two minutes; no dependencies.

### About page framing

Position around mission, not biography:

> "BudgetCraft Studio was created to make money organisation simpler. We believe financial tools
> should feel approachable, practical and easy to use — not overwhelming."

## 2. Core personality

**Floor:** practical · calm · encouraging · clear · respectful.

**Texture on top: dry, lightly funny.** Understatement and precision, not jokes.

### Dry is not jokey

✅ "Forty empty rows, twelve categories, and a formula you're apparently meant to write yourself."
❌ "Spreadsheets? Hard pass! Let's ditch the math and get organised, friend!"

The first describes the absurdity accurately. The second performs it. The second is a different
brand and would read as trying too hard.

**AI drift warning:** a request for "lightly funny" reliably produces exclamation marks, puns,
"girl, same," and relentless quipping. Dry means *fewer* words and a flatter delivery, not more
energy.

## 3. THE RECOGNITION RULE

**The central craft rule of this voice. Read this before writing anything.**

Ruling out "I" means BudgetCraft Studio can never write *"I've been there."* Against an audience
whose main barrier is shame, that is a real loss — it is the fastest way to make someone feel
less alone.

**The substitute is not warmth adjectives. It is recognition:** describing her experience so
precisely that she feels known without anyone confessing anything.

❌ "I know how it feels to open a spreadsheet and give up." — needs a founder.
❌ "We understand how overwhelming budgeting can feel." — claims empathy without demonstrating it.
✅ "You open the template, see forty empty rows, and close the laptop." — needs only accuracy.

**Specificity does the job that intimacy would otherwise do.**

**Corollary:** if a passage feels cold, the fix is **more specificity, never more punctuation.**
Never an exclamation mark, never an emoji, never "friend."

### WARMTH RULE — locked

When content feels too cold:

**Do not add:** extra emoji · exaggerated enthusiasm · jokes · motivational language
**Instead add:** specific situations · practical examples · understanding of the reader's moment

**Warmth comes from recognition, not performance.**

**Worked example:**

- ❌ Cold: "Create a better budgeting habit."
- ✅ BudgetCraft Studio: "Before the month ends, take ten minutes to check the subscriptions,
  grocery trips and small purchases that quietly added up."

The second reads as human because Sarah recognises herself in it. Nothing was added except
detail.

## 4. Humour rules

**Target the tool, the system, or the situation. Never Sarah.**

Never her spending, her decisions, her kids, her partner, her income. Per `TARGET_AUDIENCE` §7
she is shame-sensitive; humour pointed anywhere near her choices curdles instantly — she reads it
as being laughed at even when it is not.

### The off-switch — humour goes fully silent here

1. **Crisis signposting** (`TARGET_AUDIENCE` §12). Someone facing eviction gets sincerity only.
2. **The moment the number lands.** She has just totalled her spending and it stung. A joke there
   reads as making light of exactly what she was afraid of.
3. **The restart page.** *"Missed a Week? Start Here"* must be genuinely kind. Anything wry sounds
   like flippantly letting her off the hook, and undercuts the page doing the most emotional work
   in the product.

Dry in pins, blog posts and product-page copy. Sincere in the three moments above. **That
distinction is the voice** — an unbroken register of mild amusement would eventually read as not
taking her seriously.

## 5. Vocabulary rules

**BudgetCraft Studio is a translation brand, not a no-jargon brand.** The job is not to remove
financial terms. The job is to make unfamiliar terms feel simple and useful.

### Core rule

**Explain first. Name second.**

Never lead with jargon. Teach the concept, then attach the label. Sarah should never feel she
walked into a finance classroom — she should feel someone explained something clearly.

### Explain once per piece, not per mention

Define on first use, then use the term freely. Literal application to every instance turns a blog
post into a glossary — and repeated definition is also how you signal you think she didn't get it
the first time.

### Level 1 — everyday language (default)

money coming in · money going out · bills · spending · groceries · subscriptions · savings ·
monthly plan · regular expenses · extra spending · money goals

### Level 2 — permitted after explanation

| Term | Introduce as |
| --- | --- |
| **Budget** | Use freely. "A budget is simply a plan for where your money will go before you spend it." |
| **Fixed expenses** | "Bills that usually stay the same each month, like rent, insurance or a phone bill." |
| **Variable expenses** | "Spending that changes each month, like groceries, gas or eating out." |
| **Sinking fund** | "Money you set aside little by little for a future expense, like holiday gifts, car repairs or yearly subscriptions." |
| **Net income** | "The money that actually reaches your bank account after taxes and deductions." |

### Anti-condescension rule

*Sarah is not unintelligent.* She is looking for a system that does not make her feel behind. The
failure mode this guards against is **over-explaining**:

- ❌ "Don't worry, this is easier than it sounds!"
- ❌ "Even if you're not a numbers person…"
- ❌ "Math-free, we promise!"

Explain the thing. Do not reassure her that she can cope with the explanation.

### Naming pattern — searched name, translated subtitle

Search terms belong in names and titles. Translation belongs immediately underneath.

**Sinking Fund Tracker** — *money set aside, little by little, for the bills you know are coming*
**Monthly Budget Planner** — *a simple plan you can restart any week*

Do not translate the search term out of the name — an invisible product helps nobody. Do not
leave the term unexplained either. Both, in that order.

**Blog application:** search vocabulary in the title, translation in the opening line.
Title: *"What Is a Sinking Fund? A Beginner's Guide + Printable Tracker"*
Opening: *"A sinking fund sounds like a finance term. The idea is simpler than the name: small
amounts set aside over time for expenses you already know are coming."*

**Note:** "budget" is a Level 2 term marked *use freely* and is keyword priority 3 in
`TARGET_AUDIENCE` §8. Do not drop it from product names in favour of softer words — it costs
discoverability for no gain.

### Every printable introducing a term must include

1. Simple definition · 2. Real-life example · 3. How to use it · 4. Why it helps

## 6. Promise hierarchy

**BudgetCraft Studio does not have one universal promise.** The promise changes by funnel stage,
because the question in Sarah's head changes.

| Stage | Where | Lead promise | Her question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Discovery** | Pins, blog, lead magnet | **Process** — "Find out where your money goes." | *"Why is there never anything left?"* |
| **2. Purchase** | Paid system sales page | **Cost/effort** — "A simple money routine you can finish in fifteen minutes a week, and restart anytime." | *"Will I actually stick with this?"* |
| **3. Outcome** | Everywhere | **Feeling never leads.** It trails as the earned consequence. | — |

### Why stage 2 differs from stage 1

Process is the **free tracker's** promise — that is literally its job under the
diagnosis/transformation boundary in `TARGET_AUDIENCE` §9. If it also leads the paid sales page,
the paid product has no promise of its own and competes with the freebie.

The paid page leads with **effort** because Objection 1 is what blocks the purchase. She already
believes the system would work. She does not believe she would keep it up.

**Process sells the download. Effort sells the product.**

### Feeling language

- ❌ "Feel confident with money." — leads with feeling.
- ✅ "When you know what's happening with your money, decisions get easier." — shows the process
  that produces the feeling.

### Funnel logic

```
"I don't know where my money goes."
        ↓  free
"Let's help you see it."
        ↓  trust
"This was simpler than I expected."
        ↓  paid problem
"I need a routine I can actually maintain."
        ↓  paid solution
"A fifteen-minute weekly money system."
        ↓  result
"I feel more in control."
```

### Hard limits on all promises

No income claims. No guaranteed savings amounts. No financial advice. Educational content and
organisational tools only. Affiliate relationships always disclosed; commission rates and
programme terms never invented.

## 7. Mechanics

### Emoji: none. Customer-facing copy uses clean typography only.

No emoji in pins, blog posts, email subjects, email bodies, product pages or printables.
*(Internal working documents are exempt.)*

**Consequence — design now carries the warmth.** Removing emoji removes the cheap source of
friendliness, which makes the "aesthetic is a design requirement" finding in `TARGET_AUDIENCE` §8
load-bearing. A pin with no emoji and flat typography reads austere rather than calm. The images
must do that work.

### Punctuation

No exclamation marks. No all-caps for emphasis. No "…" for suspense. Em dashes and full stops
carry the rhythm.

### Sentences

**The rule is one idea per sentence.** Length follows from that.

No hard word cap — uniformly short sentences read clipped and slightly stern, which fights
everything else in this file. **The enemy is the stacked subordinate clause, not the long
sentence.**

Short by default in pins, product pages and email. Blog posts may breathe with varied rhythm.

### Address

**"You."** Never "mama," "mom," "moms," "mamas," "ladies," "friend," or "boss babe." Per
`TARGET_AUDIENCE` §1, Sarah is the primary avatar but not the only permitted reader, and those
labels shut everyone else out. Her family context belongs in the **examples**, never in the
greeting.

### Market: global. Spelling and idiom handled separately.

**The audience is global English-language, not US-only.** That splits one former rule into two,
because spelling and idiom fail differently.

**Spelling: US forms, for search.** *Organize, color, favorite, center, analyze.* This is a
keyword decision, not a cultural one — US search volume dominates English-language Pinterest, and
`PINTEREST_CONTENT_ENGINE` §6 already treats *organize* vs *organise* as a ranking issue. A reader
in Manchester or Melbourne reads "organize" without friction. A US searcher typing "organize"
never finds "organise."

**Idiom: neutral, always.** US-only words are comprehension failures outside North America, and
unlike spelling they cannot be read through:

| Avoid — US-only | Use — neutral |
| --- | --- |
| gas | fuel |
| takeout | delivery, eating out |
| diaper | nappy is also regional — say *baby supplies* |
| stroller | *baby gear* |
| closet | storage |
| vacation | trip, time off |
| check (payment) | avoid entirely |
| bangs, faucet, trash | avoid entirely |

**Currency: no symbol.** Printables and templates use an unlabelled total field so she writes her
own. Never print a currency sign, and never quote example amounts in a single currency inside a
printable.

**Region-specific claims: none.** No tax rules, no benefit schemes, no "the average American
household." Anything true only in one country is out.

Applies to product pages, printables, pins, blog posts, email, board names and category labels.
Internal working files may drift; anything Sarah reads may not.

## 8. Words we use and avoid

### Use

simple · plan · routine · restart · see · notice · already filled in · a few minutes · where your
money went · one page · start where you are · small steps count · let's make this easier

### Avoid — finance-professional register

optimise your finances · maximise your savings potential · wealth-building strategy · financial
freedom journey · money mastery · asset allocation · capital growth · cash flow optimisation ·
consumer behaviour correction · take charge of your financial future · unlock your wealth
potential · master your money mindset · achieve financial freedom

### Avoid — shame register

fix your bad money habits · stop being irresponsible · financial discipline · you need to get
serious · no more excuses · stop wasting money · where you're going wrong

### Avoid — NAMING THE JUDGMENT

**Added because this phrasing recurred three times across `LEAD_MAGNET_SYSTEM` drafting despite
the rule in §3.** It keeps returning because *no judgment* genuinely is the brand's
differentiator — but it is an **internal** positioning truth, not customer-facing copy. Saying it
aloud puts the idea in Sarah's head and invites her to supply her own verdict.

**Banned outright:** without judgment · no judgment · judgment-free · no shame · shame-free ·
guilt-free · not your spending mistakes · no blame · nobody's judging you · without the guilt

**The absence of judgment is delivered structurally, never stated:** no budget limits, no
recommended percentages, no red ink, no scoring, no deficit totals, and a reflection prompt that
asks what surprised her rather than where she went wrong. If those are all in place, she will
feel it. If they are not, saying it will not help.

**Screening question before any headline or subtitle ships:** does this sentence mention a
negative thing in order to deny it? If yes, cut the negative and let the design prove the point.

### Avoid — FOUNDER VOICE

**Added because this recurred twice during `LEAD_MAGNET_SYSTEM` and `PRODUCT_CREATION` drafting
despite the rule in §1.** It slips through because first-person copy is the default register of
this entire niche.

**Banned outright:** I wish I had · I used to · when I was · I tried · I created · I built · I
paid off · my own experience · what worked for me · I know how it feels · we've all been there ·
take it from someone who

**The tell:** any construction implying a person with a financial history. BudgetCraft Studio has
no founder story and will not invent one.

**Rewrite pattern:**

- ❌ "The simple spending tracker I wish I had."
- ✅ "The simple spending tracker nobody hands you."
- ❌ "This is why I created the budget system."
- ✅ "It's why BudgetCraft Studio exists."

### Avoid — false-veteran register

Per `TARGET_AUDIENCE` §7, Sarah has failed at **one** thing: a spreadsheet. Never imply a history
of failed attempts.

- ❌ "You've downloaded every budgeting app and none of them stuck."
- ❌ "Another planner destined for the drawer."
- ❌ "You've tried everything."
- ✅ "Simpler than the spreadsheet that defeated you."

### Pin titles

**Good**

- Where Did My Money Go? A Free Monthly Tracker
- A Budget You Can Restart Any Week
- Last Month's Spending, on One Page
- No Spreadsheets. Twelve Categories Already Filled In.

**Bad**

- Take Charge of Your Financial Future — *finance-influencer register*
- Unlock Your Wealth Potential — *banned phrase*
- Save $500 This Month — *guaranteed savings claim*
- Girl, Let's Ditch That Spreadsheet! — *jokey, exclamation, "girl"*

### Blog opening

**Good**

> You open the template someone recommended. Forty empty rows, twelve categories you're meant to
> name yourself, and a formula you're apparently supposed to write. You close the laptop.
>
> Nothing about that means you're bad with money. It means the tool asked you to build the tool.

*Recognition rule doing the work. Dry, specific, warm without a single warm word. The enemy is
named as the spreadsheet.*

**Bad**

> Are you ready to take control of your finances? Budgeting doesn't have to be scary! Even if
> you're not a numbers person, you can master your money with these simple tips.

*Exclamation, over-reassurance, condescension, three banned phrases.*

### Paid system sales page opening

**Good**

> Fifteen minutes on a Sunday. That's the routine.
>
> Every page is undated, so there's no wrong month to start. There's a page for the week you
> miss, because there will be one.

*Leads with effort, per §6. Answers Objection 1 before she raises it.*

**Bad**

> Transform your finances with our complete budgeting system. Finally achieve the financial
> freedom you deserve.

*Leads with feeling, two banned phrases, promises an outcome.*

### The restart page — humour off

**Good**

> **Missed a week? Start here.**
>
> Pick up from today. You don't need to fill in the pages you skipped, and nothing here is
> ruined. This page exists because missed weeks are normal.

*Sincere, no wryness, no absolution-as-joke.*

**Bad**

> Fell off the wagon? We've all been there! Dust yourself off and jump back in.

*"We've all been there" is a founder claim. Cliché. Flippant on the wrong page.*

### The moment the number lands — humour off

**Good**

> That's your total for the month. Which number surprised you most?

*Information, not verdict. One question, no commentary. Per `TARGET_AUDIENCE` §7.*

**Bad**

> Ouch — $412 over! Time to rein in that takeaway habit.

*Verdict, scary total, targets her spending, jokes at the wrong moment.*

### Email subject lines

**Good:** Where did last month's money go? · The week you miss is already in the plan · One page,
twelve categories, already filled in

**Bad:** Ready to transform your finances?! · Last chance to unlock financial freedom · Your money
mindset makeover starts now

## 10. Decision filters

Every piece of copy passes all four:

1. **Would Sarah understand this without looking anything up?**
2. **Does this blame the tool rather than her?**
3. **Is the promise the right one for this funnel stage?** (§6)
4. **Is the warmth coming from specificity rather than punctuation?** (§3)

And the parent filter from `TARGET_AUDIENCE` §13:

**"Will this help Sarah feel more confident, organised, or in control of her money today?"**
